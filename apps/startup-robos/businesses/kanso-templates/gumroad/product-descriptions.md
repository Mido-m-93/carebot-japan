**Full Product Description:**

---

Most Japan travel guides try to be encyclopedias.

This bundle is not an encyclopedia.

It's three tools — each one covering exactly one job. Together, they handle the full planning process for a 14-day trip from Tokyo to Osaka.

---

**Tool 1: The 14-Day Itinerary**

Tokyo (6 days) → Kyoto (4 days) → Osaka (3 days) → departure

The most logical routing for a first-time Japan trip, built around what's actually worth your time:

- Tokyo: Asakusa, Harajuku, Shibuya, Akihabara, Nikko day trip, Ginza, Tsukiji
- Kyoto: Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama bamboo grove, Gion, Nara day trip
- Osaka: Dotonbori, Shinsekai, Hiroshima + Miyajima option

Each day has a theme, a morning/afternoon/evening structure, and honest notes about what's crowded and what's worth waking up early for.

---

**Tool 2: The Packing System**

Not a packing list — a system.

Built on a simple rule: carry-on only, even for 14 days. Japan's coin lockers, clean laundromats, and world-class convenience stores mean you never actually need a checked bag.

What's inside:
- Master clothing list organized by category
- Day pack checklist (what to carry every day)
- Documents and cards checklist
- Electronics list (what matters, what doesn't)
- Health and toiletries guide — with a note on what to just buy in Japan

Includes the "Kanso Packing Philosophy" — a short framework for deciding what actually goes in the bag.

---

**Tool 3: The Transport Decoder**

Japan's train system looks complicated. It isn't, once you understand three things:

1. IC Cards (Suica/Pasmo)
2. The JR Pass — whether it's worth it for your trip
3. The logic of Shinkansen types

This guide covers:
- The IC card system — how it works, how to load it, where it's accepted
- JR Pass math — a cost breakdown so you can calculate whether to buy one
- Shinkansen types explained (Nozomi vs Hikari vs Kodama)
- Tokyo Metro vs JR — which to use for which area
- Kyoto bus vs subway cheat sheet
- Key transport phrases in Japanese
- Emergency contacts and essential apps
