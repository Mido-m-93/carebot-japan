# Osaka to Hiroshima: Which Route, Which Pass

The Osaka–Hiroshima corridor is one of Japan's most-traveled stretches, and it's also one of the most confusing when it comes to passes and tickets. JR Pass? Sanyo Pass? Just buy a ticket? I've done this route more times than I can count — day trips, overnight stays, and once with a suitcase I deeply regretted — so here's the honest breakdown.

---

## How Far Is It and How Long Does It Take?

Osaka (Shin-Osaka station) to Hiroshima is about 340 km by Shinkansen. On the **Nozomi**, the fastest Shinkansen on the Tokaido/Sanyo line, the trip takes roughly **85 minutes**. On the **Hikari**, which the JR Pass covers, expect around **100–110 minutes** — still fast, still comfortable.

If you're coming from Osaka's city center, add 15–20 minutes to reach Shin-Osaka by subway from Namba or Umeda.

The Kodama stops at every station and takes about 3 hours. There's almost no reason to take it unless you're doing a specific hop.

---

## Shinkansen Ticket Options: What Actually Makes Sense

This is where most travelers overthink it.

**Without a pass**, a reserved seat on the Hikari from Shin-Osaka to Hiroshima costs around ¥10,440 one-way (as of 2026). Round trip is ¥20,880. If you're only going Osaka↔Hiroshima and nowhere else, buying individual tickets is fine — no pass math required.

**With the national JR Pass**, the Hiroshima leg is fully covered — both directions, any Hikari or Kodama you want. If you're already traveling Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka–Hiroshima–(back), the JR Pass math starts to work in your favor. [Buy the JR Pass before you arrive in Japan →][https://www.jrpass.com/]

**With the Sanyo-San'in Area Pass**, you get unlimited travel on Shinkansen between Osaka and Hiroshima (plus Kyushu routes and the Kinosaki onsen area) for 7 days at a lower price than the national JR Pass. This is the smarter pick if your trip is focused on western Japan and you're not heading east of Kyoto.

A quick rule: if your itinerary includes Tokyo, get the national JR Pass. If it's Osaka/Kyoto west only, the regional pass wins.

## What to Know Once You're in Hiroshima

Hiroshima station is on the JR Sanyo main line and is well-connected. The tram (streetcar) system gets you from the station to the Peace Memorial Park in about 15 minutes for ¥180 — no IC card tricks needed, just pay when you exit.

Miyajima access: Take the JR Sanyo line from Hiroshima to Miyajimaguchi (25 min, covered by JR Pass), then the Miyajima Ferry (also JR Pass-eligible on the JR-operated ferry — not the competing one). This is one of the few ferry legs where the JR Pass actually saves you meaningful money.

For connectivity while you're out there — Miyajima has spotty signal in places — make sure you have a working eSIM or pocket WiFi before you leave Osaka. See [our eSIM guide](/best-esim-japan-2026) for what actually works in rural Hiroshima prefecture.

## FAQ

<!-- FAQ_SCHEMA_START -->

**Q: Can I use the JR Pass on the Nozomi Shinkansen from Osaka to Hiroshima?**
A: No. The Nozomi (and Mizuho) are excluded from the JR Pass. You'll need to take the Hikari or Kodama. The Hikari is fast enough — about 100 minutes — and runs frequently throughout the day.

**Q: Is it worth doing Hiroshima as a day trip from Osaka?**
A: Yes, if you plan it tight. Leave Osaka by 8am, you'll have 6–7 hours in Hiroshima before a comfortable return. That's enough for the Peace Memorial Museum and Miyajima — not both leisurely, but both doable.

**Q: Do I need to reserve a seat on the Shinkansen?**
A: With a JR Pass, seat reservations are free and strongly recommended, especially on weekends and Golden Week. You can also ride unreserved cars, but reserved seats are available at JR ticket machines or the green counter — book the morning of at minimum.
