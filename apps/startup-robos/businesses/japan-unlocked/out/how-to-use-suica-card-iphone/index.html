# How to Use a Suica Card on iPhone and Apple Watch

If you have an iPhone 7 or later and are visiting Japan, you do not need to carry a physical Suica card. You can load one directly into Apple Wallet, tap through every train gate in the country, and pay at convenience stores without touching your wallet once. I have been doing this for years and it is genuinely one of the best quality-of-life upgrades for getting around Japan.

Here is exactly how to set it up, top it up, and avoid the common mistakes.

---

## What You Need Before You Start

Before opening Apple Wallet, make sure you have the following:

- **iPhone 7 or later** (or Apple Watch Series 3 or later)
- **iOS 10.1 or later** (iOS 16+ recommended)
- **A non-Japanese Apple ID is fine** — this works in any region now
- **A credit or debit card linked to Apple Pay** for topping up
- **Mobile data or Wi-Fi** during the initial setup

One important note: your phone does not need to be connected to the internet to *use* Suica after setup. The card data lives on the device's secure chip. You only need connectivity when adding money.

---

## How to Add Suica to Your iPhone Wallet

1. Open the **Wallet** app on your iPhone
2. Tap the **+** button in the top right corner
3. Select **Transit Card**
4. Choose **Suica** from the list
5. Enter the amount you want to load (minimum ¥1,000 is a safe starting point for a few days)
6. Confirm with Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode
7. Wait about 10–15 seconds for the card to be issued

That is it. Your Suica is active immediately. No post office, no machine, no queue.

If you are adding Suica to an **Apple Watch**, open the Watch app on your iPhone, go to Wallet & Apple Pay, and follow the same steps. The card lives on the watch independently — useful when you want to leave your phone in your bag.

**Already have a physical Suica card?** You can transfer the balance from a physical card to iPhone at any JR East station using a green ticket machine. Ask a station attendant if you cannot find the option — they deal with this daily.

---

## Using Suica at Train Gates and Shops

Hold your iPhone near the IC card reader with the screen face-up. You do not need to unlock your phone or open any app. The same applies to Apple Watch — raise your wrist, tap the reader.

Suica works on:

- JR trains across Japan (Shinkansen excluded — see [our Shinkansen guide](/tokyo-to-kyoto-shinkansen) for how to book those)
- Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway
- Most private railways (Hankyu, Odakyu, Keikyu, etc.)
- Buses in most major cities
- Convenience stores: 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson
- Vending machines, some taxis, and airport express trains including the Narita Express (check [our Narita guide](/narita-airport-to-tokyo) for transport options from the airport)

The card deducts the exact fare automatically. No need to know prices in advance.

---

## How to Top Up Suica on iPhone

You can add money to your Suica in three ways:

1. **In the Wallet app**: Tap your Suica card, tap Add Money, choose an amount, confirm with Apple Pay. Done in under a minute with mobile data.
2. **At a JR ticket machine**: Select "Charge IC Card" on the touch screen, hold your phone to the reader, insert cash. Works offline.
3. **At convenience store registers**: Ask the cashier to charge your IC card and hold your phone to their reader.

I keep at least ¥2,000 on mine at all times. Running out at a ticket gate is avoidable — the machine will let you top up on the spot, but it slows you down during rush hour.

## FAQ

<!-- FAQ_SCHEMA_START -->

**Q: Can I use Suica on iPhone outside Japan?**
A: No. Suica only works on Japanese transit and payment infrastructure. The card stays active on your device, but you can only use it when you are physically in Japan.

**Q: What happens if my iPhone battery dies at a train gate?**
A: iPhone has a feature called Express Transit which reserves a small amount of battery for Suica after the phone powers off. You typically get a few more taps after the low battery warning. Do not rely on this — charge your phone.

**Q: Can I get a refund on my Suica balance when I leave Japan?**
A: Yes, but only at a JR East station ticket machine or service counter. There is a ¥220 handling fee deducted from the refund. If your remaining balance is small, it may be easier to spend it at a convenience store before you leave.
