# How to Buy a JR Pass Outside Japan: Step-by-Step

If you are planning more than one long-distance train trip in Japan, the JR Pass will almost certainly save you money. But there is a catch most first-timers miss: you can only buy it outside Japan, before you board your flight. Once you land at Narita or Haneda, the window closes.

This guide walks you through exactly how to buy one, which type to choose, and what to do when you arrive.

---

## Why You Can Only Buy It Outside Japan

The JR Pass is a product designed for overseas visitors. Japan made it available exclusively through foreign sales channels as part of its tourism policy. Until 2023 there was a brief in-Japan pilot, but it was discontinued after prices were raised to discourage it.

The practical result: you need to buy your pass from an authorized reseller in your home country before departure. Do it at least a few days before your trip — not the night before — because physical exchange orders need shipping time, and even digital orders benefit from having a buffer.

---

## Step 1 — Decide Which JR Pass You Need

There are two core decisions: coverage area and duration.

**Coverage:**
- **Nationwide JR Pass** — covers the entire JR network including the Shinkansen (except Nozomi and Mizuho bullet trains). Best if you are hopping between Tokyo, Kyoto, Hiroshima, and beyond.
- **Regional passes** — JR West Kansai Pass, JR Kyushu Rail Pass, and others. Cheaper if your trip is geographically concentrated.

**Duration:** 7, 14, or 21 days. The clock starts on the day you activate it at the JR office in Japan, not the day you buy it. So buy it whenever you want — but plan your activation date carefully.

For a classic Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka–Hiroshima loop, the 7-day Nationwide pass pays for itself in roughly 1.5 round trips on the Tokaido Shinkansen. You can verify this using the route calculator on most reseller sites.

## Step 2 — Choose a Reseller and Purchase

The most reliable authorized resellers include:

- **JRPass.com** — [https://www.jrpass.com/] — one of the largest dedicated resellers, ships physical exchange orders and offers digital options
- **Japan Experience** — [https://www.japan-experience.com/rail-passes] — another long-standing operator with solid customer support
- **Klook / Viator** — useful for last-minute digital vouchers, though confirm they are JR-authorized before buying

What to look for in a reseller:

1. Clear statement that they are an **authorized JR Pass reseller**
2. Option for digital or physical exchange order (both are valid)
3. A transparent refund policy in case your travel plans change before activation

Avoid random third-party marketplace listings. Counterfeit JR Pass vouchers do circulate, and the penalty for using one unknowingly at a JR gate is embarrassing and expensive.

---

## Step 3 — Receive Your Exchange Order

After purchase you will receive either:

- **A physical MCO (exchange order)** mailed to your home address. This is a paper document that looks underwhelming for its price. Keep it somewhere safe — lose it and you lose the pass.
- **A digital voucher / QR code** — increasingly common and easier to manage. Save it to your phone and back it up to email.

Both options are exchanged for the actual JR Pass card at a JR Travel Service Center inside Japan. The exchange itself takes about five minutes.

---

## Step 4 — Activate It After You Land

When you arrive in Japan, go to a JR Travel Service Center before your first train journey. These are located at all major airports including Narita (see [our Narita guide](/narita-airport-to-tokyo) for exactly where to find it) and Haneda, plus major stations like Shinjuku and Kyoto.

Hand over your exchange order or show your digital voucher. Staff will ask for your passport and your desired **activation date**. This is important: you do not have to activate it on the day you exchange it. If you are spending your first two days in Tokyo before heading to Kyoto, set the activation date to coincide with your first Shinkansen ride.

Once activated, the pass is a physical IC-style card. Tap it at the green JR-only gates — not the standard gates — and walk through.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I buy a JR Pass after arriving in Japan?**
No. The JR Pass for foreign tourists is only sold outside Japan through authorized overseas resellers. There was a short-lived in-Japan sales trial, but it ended in 2023. Purchase before you fly.

**How far in advance should I buy my JR Pass?**
If you are getting a physical exchange order, order at least 7–10 days before departure to allow for shipping. Digital vouchers can be purchased days or even hours before you land, but earlier is always safer.

**What happens if I miss my activation window?**
The exchange order itself does not expire quickly, but confirm the validity period with your reseller. Once you exchange it for the physical pass and set an activation date, that countdown is final — unused days are not refunded.
